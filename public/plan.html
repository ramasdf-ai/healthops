Rotation Strength Plan

Muscle, focus and mood, built around a 3‑2‑2‑3 custody rotation. Kid‑free days carry the real training. Kid days keep a short version so the habit never breaks. Oura and Garmin tell you when to push and when to back off.

Built for: 143 lb, 5′6″, newer to lifting, training at home with adjustable dumbbells (15 lb max each), a bike trainer and a yoga mat, no conditions or diet limits, daily finasteride, oral minoxidil and a hair supplement chew. The rotation is a 10‑day loop (3 with kids, 2 free, 2 with kids, 3 free). Evidence tags: strong = consistent trials or meta‑analyses, moderate = good but mixed, practical = coaching convention.

The core idea

Fixed weekday programs break on a rotation. This plan runs a session queue instead. On a free day you do the next full session in the list, whatever day it is. On a kid day you do a 20‑minute micro session or a walk. Missed days don't need making up, because the queue simply waits.

Free day

  • Next full session from the queue, 45 to 60 min at home
  • One 90‑minute deep‑work block
  • Batch‑cook protein for the next kid block
  • One social plan per free block

Kid day

  • 20‑min micro session, or a 30‑min walk with the kids
  • Protein anchors already prepped, no cooking decisions
  • One 30 to 45‑min focus block, if any
  • Same wake time as free days

Your hair meds and this plan

None of them get in the way of building muscle. A few things to know:

  • Testosterone test: tell the doctor you take finasteride. It blocks the conversion of testosterone to DHT, so testosterone often reads a little higher than it otherwise would. strong
  • Supplement chew: check the label for biotin. Biotin can throw off many lab tests, including testosterone and thyroid tests. Skip the chew for 3 days before any blood draw. strong
  • Finasteride and mood: the FDA label lists depression and, rarely, suicidal thoughts as possible side effects. Your daily mood and stress ratings will catch this early. If mood drops and stays down, tell your prescriber before blaming the training. strong
  • Finasteride and muscle: no good evidence that it slows muscle gain. Muscle growth runs mainly on testosterone, not DHT. moderate
  • Minoxidil: topical (foam or liquid) barely reaches the rest of the body. Oral minoxidil is a blood pressure drug. It can raise resting heart rate, which Oura will show, cause lightheadedness when standing up fast, and add water weight, which can blur your weekly weight trend. Since you take it by mouth, get up slowly between floor exercises and tell your doctor about any swelling. You're already on it, so your Nov–Dec baseline includes its effect on resting heart rate. Oura stays comparable as long as the dose doesn't change. Log any dose change in your daily check‑in. strong
  • Creatine and hair: a single small 2009 study found creatine raised DHT. Later studies haven't repeated that finding, and finasteride blocks that pathway anyway. Most people on finasteride can take it, but ask your prescriber if you want to be sure. moderate

Prep for the draw

  • Fast 8 to 12 hours, water only. Get drawn between 7 and 10 am.
  • Skip the hair supplement chew for 3 days, because biotin skews testosterone and thyroid results.
  • No hard workout for 48 hours before, since heavy training temporarily raises liver and muscle enzymes.
  • No alcohol the day before. Sleep normally.

Your next 20 days

Each 10‑day loop gives 5 free days: 4 full sessions plus 1 rest day, and 2 to 3 micro sessions on kid days.

Training

As a newer lifter you'll grow fastest on two full‑body sessions that alternate. Per 10 days that's 4 full sessions plus 2 to 3 micro sessions, so every muscle gets trained about three times a week. For someone early in training, that frequency and volume is plenty. strong

Full sessions (free days, in order)

QueueSessionMain liftsSets × reps
AFull body ABulgarian split squat holding both DBs, push‑up (raise your feet as it gets easy), one‑arm DB row (3‑sec lower, 1‑sec pause at the top), single‑leg DB Romanian deadlift, plank3 × 10–20
BFull body BGoblet squat (3‑sec lower, 1½ reps), pull‑up or inverted row under a sturdy table, seated DB overhead press, single‑leg hip thrust with a DB, curl + overhead triceps extension3 × 10–20
  • Take most sets to within 0 to 2 reps of failure. Dumbbells cap the weight, so effort and reps do the work. Sets of 8 to 20 build muscle as well as heavy sets when taken that close to failure. strong
  • Progress by adding a rep, then weight once you hit the top of the range on every set. Garmin's strength activity logs sets and reps; note top‑set weight in the activity title.
  • Working around 15 lb: that's light for legs and back, so the sessions use one‑leg moves, slow lowering, pauses and 1½ reps to make it harder. Once you hit 20 clean reps on a move, make the next version harder rather than adding weight. A doorway pull‑up bar is now the most useful thing to buy. Expect legs to outgrow the dumbbells within 2 to 3 months. At that point, the next upgrade is an adjustable set that goes to 50 lb.
  • On the third day of a 3‑day free block, rest or ride the bike trainer for 30 to 40 easy minutes (zone 2, logged by Garmin). Cardio at that dose doesn't block muscle gain. moderate

Micro session (kid days, 20 min, home)

Three rounds: goblet squat ×12, push‑up ×max−2, one‑arm DB row ×12/side, reverse lunge ×10/side, dead bug ×10. A walk or bike ride with the kids counts just as well. The goal is keeping the habit alive, not adding load.

Pick a starting dose

Low risk

Minimum

3 full sessions per 10 days (A, B, then alternate). Pick this if you're returning from a layoff or sleep is poor. Slower gains, almost no chance of burnout.

Recommended

Standard

4 full sessions plus micro sessions, as above. Best ratio of results to schedule strain for a parent on a rotation.

Higher reward

Push

Train all 5 free days and do micro sessions on every kid day. Only if readiness stays above 75 for two weeks straight. Recovery debt shows up first in HRV.

Readiness rules

Check Oura in the morning before deciding the day. Use the readiness score as a gate, and use the trends (HRV, resting heart rate, temperature) to confirm it. A single low score matters less than two in a row.

Nutrition for muscle

  • Protein: about 105 to 130 g a day, which is 1.6 to 2 g per kg at 143 lb. Benefits level off around 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg, so going higher mostly costs money. strong
  • Spread it: 3 to 4 meals of about 30 to 40 g each. moderate
  • Calories: since you're fine adding some fat, eat about 400 to 500 kcal over maintenance and aim for a gain of 0.5 to 0.75 lb a week on the 7‑day average. Newer lifters build muscle fastest, and this pace takes advantage of that. If the average climbs faster than 1 lb a week, cut back on carbs and fats, never protein. If it stalls for two weeks, add about 200 kcal a day, such as a glass of whole milk and a banana. moderate
  • Creatine monohydrate: 3 to 5 g daily, any time, no loading. It's the best‑supported supplement for strength and lean mass. strong Check with a doctor first if you have kidney issues.
  • Kid‑day anchors: Greek yogurt, eggs, rotisserie chicken, tuna or salmon pouches, cottage cheese, a protein shake. Stock them during the free block so kid days need zero planning.

Focus

  • Sleep comes first. Aim for 7 to 9 hours and keep the same wake time on kid and free days. Irregular sleep schedules hurt attention and mood independently of total hours. strong
  • Morning light: 10 minutes outside within an hour of waking anchors your body clock. moderate
  • Deep‑work block: 90 minutes on free days, 30 to 45 on kid days, with the phone in another room. Schedule it, don't wait for the mood. practical
  • Caffeine: none after about 1 pm. Late caffeine shortens deep sleep, and Oura will show it. strong
  • Training helps directly. A session or brisk walk sharpens attention for a few hours afterward, so put training before your hardest work when you can. moderate
  • Bacopa (Jan 1 – Feb 28): 300 mg a day of an extract standardized to about 50% bacosides, taken with food. Trials show modest gains in memory recall after 8 to 12 weeks, with little effect before then. Common side effects are an upset stomach and drowsiness, so take it with dinner if it makes you sleepy. Buy a third‑party‑tested brand, because some products have tested high for heavy metals. moderate
  • CDP‑choline (citicoline), optional, March or later: 250 to 500 mg in the morning. Evidence in healthy adults under 50 is thin, coming from a few small trials on attention. Eggs already supply choline. Add it only after the bacopa trial so you can tell which one is doing what. limited
  • One change at a time: training, creatine and sleep fixes all start in November. If you start the supplements at the same time, you won't know what moved your brain‑fog score.

Emotional wellbeing

  • Exercise counts as treatment. Strength training and walking both reduce depressive symptoms in large meta‑analyses, with effects comparable to standard therapies. This plan already covers it. strong
  • Handoff ritual: handoff days can bring a dip, especially the first evening of an empty house. Put a planned anchor there, such as a training session, a call with a friend, or a meal out. practical
  • One social touch per free block, in person when possible. Loneliness predicts low mood more strongly than most lifestyle factors. strong
  • Daily 30‑second check‑in: rate mood 1 to 5 in Oura's tags or a notes app. It gives us a trend to read alongside sleep and training.
  • Kid days: outdoor time with the kids counts as recovery, movement and connection at once.

Prediction to log now

Before you start, write down what you expect after 12 weeks (pounds gained, top‑set increase on squat and bench) and how confident you are, as a percentage. We'll check it at week 12 so you can see how well your own forecasts hold up.

For reference, the target pace works out to about 6 to 9 lb on the scale over 12 weeks, part muscle and part fat. Rep counts usually climb quickly in the first few months.

See a doctor if

  • Chest pain, fainting, unusual breathlessness or palpitations during exercise. Stop and get checked before training again.
  • You have heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes or kidney issues, or take regular medication. Get clearance before increasing training or starting creatine.
  • Oura flags a high temperature deviation for several days, or readiness stays under 60 for a week without an obvious cause.
  • Loud snoring, gasping at night, or frequent breathing disturbances in Oura. Ask about a sleep apnea screen.
  • Lightheadedness, a racing heart, or swelling in the ankles or face, if you take oral minoxidil.
  • Low mood, loss of interest or poor sleep lasting more than two weeks. Mention finasteride, since mood changes are a listed side effect. Talk to a doctor or therapist. If you ever have thoughts of self‑harm, call or text 988 (US).
  • Joint pain that lasts past 48 hours or changes how you move.
  • Testosterone comes back low. Treatment decisions belong to your doctor, and over‑the‑counter "T boosters" aren't worth the money or the risk.

No physical in the last year? Book one with basic labs (lipids, A1c, blood pressure) as your baseline.

What done looks like for v1

Run one full 10‑day loop as written. Success means 4 full sessions logged in Garmin, protein hit on at least 7 of 10 days, and the readiness rules used every morning. Then we tune volume, calories and cutoffs from your real data. Don't refine the plan further before that loop; more planning now would add little.
